# Antiquated Armor

These defenses belong to earlier warfare. They may survive as heirlooms, old colonial stores, grave goods, tournament harness, or equipment in a deliberately archaic region, but they are not ordinary purchases in the 17th-century game.

Armor Rating, Proof and Enc work exactly as described on the main [Armor](Armor.md) page.

None of these is proofed, and that is the whole point of them. A full harness is the finest protection in the book against a sword, an axe or a lance, and a pistol ball goes through it. The armourers who answered that question by thickening and proving the plate produced the harnesses on the main page; the men who did not stopped wearing armour at all.

A Referee may allow a surviving harness that was deliberately reinforced and proved, counting as Partial proof. Such a thing is exceptionally heavy and exceptionally rare, and costs at least four times the listed price.

A pavise protects against non-firearm missiles while carried. Planted and properly braced it is terrain, and may instead provide [cover](../Encounters/Combat%20Actions.md#cover); whether it stops a bullet depends on how it was built.
