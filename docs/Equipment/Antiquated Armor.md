# Antiquated Armor

These defenses belong to earlier warfare. They may survive as heirlooms, old colonial stores, grave goods, tournament harness, or equipment in a deliberately archaic region, but they are not ordinary purchases in the settled pike-and-shot warfare the main lists assume.

**In a campaign set early in the age** these are not antiques. At Pavia the gendarmes who rode down on the arquebusiers wore full harness, and it was that day's lesson — not any change of fashion — that began the long argument this page and the [Armor](Armor.md) page are both about.

Armor Rating, Proof and Enc work exactly as described on the main [Armor](Armor.md) page.

Almost none of these is proofed, and that is the whole point of them. A full harness is the finest protection in the book against a sword, an axe or a lance, and a pistol ball goes through it. The armourers who answered that question by thickening and proving the plate produced the harnesses on the main page; the men who did not stopped wearing armour at all.

The one exception is the **full harness with a proved breast** — a late field armour from the generation when armourers had begun shooting their own work but had not yet given up on covering the whole man. Only the breastplate was ever proved; the rest of the harness is the same steel as anything else here, which is why it counts as Partial and not Full. It is heavier than a purpose-built cuirassier harness and protects worse against a shot, and both of those facts are the reason the cuirassier harness exists. A Referee should treat one as a findable object rather than a purchase: the listed price is what it would cost if anyone were still making them.

A pavise protects against non-firearm missiles while carried. Planted and properly braced it is terrain, and may instead provide [cover](../Encounters/Combat%20Actions.md#cover); whether it stops a bullet depends on how it was built.
