**Armour Rating** is added to Armour Class. For every harness the rating is the same in melee and at range, which is why the table gives it once; only the shields — buckler, target, target of proof — differ between the two, and add to Melee or Ranged AC as listed. For the full attack procedure, refer to [Combat Actions](../Encounters/Combat%20Actions.md#attack).

**Firearm** is what the armour is worth against a ball. Against a firearm the AC is 10 + this number, and nothing else.

**Enc** is the encumbrance the harness costs its wearer while worn, per [Time and Movement](../Adventuring/Time%20and%20Movement.md): metal armour is +1 Point, and three-quarter or heavier is +2.

The **padded doublet** is what the Germans call a *Wams*: a quilted coat of linen and wadding, worn on its own by the poor and under plate by everyone else.

## Against a Firearm

A gun does not care how well a man is dressed. A ball goes through mail, through a jack, through a brigandine and through an ordinary harness as though they were linen — which is why the armourer's answer was to make the plate thicker and then shoot it himself to prove it. A **proof dent** is the mark of that test: evidence that this plate stopped that ball on that day. The Firearm column says what each harness is worth once the shooting starts: nothing at all for most, a little for a proved breast over an ordinary back, all of it for a harness made against the gun.

Everything else — swords, pikes, bows, crossbows, thrown weapons, falling rocks — meets the ordinary Armour Rating.

A **buckler** is the small fist-gripped shield worn at the belt, for the sword. A **target** is the larger round shield strapped to the forearm — the Italian rotella, the Spanish rodela, the Highland targe. It need not be steel: wood faced with hide stops a sword perfectly well, and stops nothing else.

A **target of proof** is thick plate, made heavy on purpose and shot at to prove it. It is the only shield that counts for anything against a firearm, it costs twenty times what an ordinary target costs, and it weighs what it has to weigh. Ordinary targets and every buckler do nothing against a shot. A planted mantlet, a stout wall, or any other substantial barrier instead grants [cover](../Encounters/Combat%20Actions.md#cover), which always helps.

The proof was a promise about a test, not about every gun at every distance. A Referee may rule that a harness made against the gun, struck at point-blank range by a musket, counts for half, and that a ball from a rifled barrel goes through it.

## Wearing It

**Jack chains** are splints riveted to a padded jack — the ordinary infantry defence of the age's first half, and what a Border reiver still wears long after. A **mail shirt** never goes out of use at all; men wear it under plate, over a jack, and by itself where nothing better can be had. A **brigandine** is small plates riveted inside a cloth garment, good armour in 1525 and old-fashioned by 1600.

A **breastplate** worn alone is usually a proved one, and the breast is where a shot is aimed, so it keeps most of its rating against a ball. **Pikeman's armour** is a helmet, breast- and backplate, and tassets, of lighter plate than a horseman's; it keeps half. **Harquebusier armour** is a buff coat under a helmet and proved breast- and backplate — the ordinary dress of this age's cavalry. **Three-quarter cuirassier armour** is a heavy proofed harness covering the wearer to the knees, and it is as unpopular with the men who wear it as its weight suggests.

The **full harness** is the complete suit of plate that rode at Pavia and lost. Nothing in the book is better against a sword and nothing is worse against a gun, and the hundred and twenty years this game covers are largely the story of men working that out. The **full harness with a proved breast** is the last attempt to keep the whole man in steel: a late armour whose breastplate was shot at and passed, with the rest of it the same metal as ever, which is why it is worth only half its rating against a ball. It is heavier than a purpose-built cuirassier harness and worse against a ball, and both of those facts are why the cuirassier harness won.

Armour pieces do not stack: use the best complete protection actually worn.
