!!! info "Training"
    The weapons marked **Training** do nothing in untaught hands. A character with no plausible background with one must spend time learning it before it can be used effectively; the Referee decides what a character's background already covers. Crossbows, darts, rocks and ordinary thrown weapons need no instruction — a crossbow is aimed like a gun and anyone can throw a hatchet badly.

!!! info "Range"
    Medium range imposes a -2 penalty to hit; Long range imposes -4.

!!! note "Ammunition"
    Arrows and crossbow bolts cost **5bp** each. Sling bullets cost **2bp** each. A quiver and up to twenty arrows or bolts count as one item for encumbrance.

### Weapon Traits

| Weapon | Effects |
| --- | --- |
| Light crossbow | Fire every other Round; ignore 1 Armor Rating. |
| Heavy crossbow | Fire every third Round; ignore 3 Armor Rating. |
| Sling | May use stones as ammunition, halving effective range. |

A crossbow is not a firearm and does not care about [Proof](../Armor.md#proof): it reduces Armor Rating by the amount listed and the rest applies normally.

The two crossbows are told apart by how they are spanned. The **light crossbow** is drawn with a goat's-foot lever, quickly enough to be worked from the saddle; it is the mounted crossbowman's arm in the opening years of the age, and afterwards the gentleman's and the gamekeeper's for birds and small game. The **stonebow**, which shoots a lead or clay pellet in place of a bolt and is the boy's and the poacher's weapon, counts as a light crossbow. The **heavy crossbow** has a steel bow too strong for any lever and is wound back with a cranequin, the German rack-and-pinion winder, or an English windlass of cords and pulleys. In the Italian wars a few companies still carry it; for the rest of the age it is a hunting and a target-shooting weapon, and the finest of them are made in Germany for princes' hunts. Neither needs the long practice of a bow, which is the whole reason it was ever carried to war.

The **sling** is a shepherd's weapon and a boy's, carried by men who would never pay for anything better. It is cheap, silent, and fed from any riverbed, and in practised hands it still breaks a skull.

A **dart or javelin** is thrown by men who fight light and fast: the Irish kern with a sheaf of darts, the Spanish jinete with his javelins, the Ottoman and Balkan light horse, and boarding parties throwing weighted or burning darts down onto an enemy's deck.

The **longbow** is the heavy military warbow, and it is on its way out rather than gone. English statute still requires practice at the butts, bows are still mustered with the trained bands into the 1590s, and there were archers on the field at Tippermuir in 1644. What killed it was never that the musket shot further or harder — it does neither — but that it takes a lifetime to make an archer and six weeks to make a musketeer. A Referee running the age's later decades should treat one as a countryman's or a huntsman's weapon rather than a soldier's.

The ordinary **Bow** entry covers hunting bows, and the bows of peoples who never gave them up.

Weapons that belong to other ages or other continents — the blowgun among them — appear under [Curious Weapons](Curious%20Weapons.md).
