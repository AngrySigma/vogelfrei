# Wilderness Travel

Beyond the walls, time is counted in watches. Distances covered, terrain, weather, and encumbrance are handled in [Time and Movement](Time%20and%20Movement.md); finding food and water is the [Bushcraft](Skills.md#bushcraft) skill. This page gives the day its structure — and its ways of going wrong.

## The Day in Watches

The day is divided into watches: the party's **active watches**, set by the Encumbrance of its most burdened member (see [Movement](Time%20and%20Movement.md#movement)), followed by **two watches of rest**. An unencumbered party has four active watches, a heavily encumbered one only two. A day always holds all its watches, so the fewer there are, the longer each one runs.

In each watch, each Character takes one action. A party usually acts as one — travelling, exploring, or foraging together — but it may divide, with some foraging while others scout ahead or guard the camp. The Referee resolves each group in turn. A divided party is a weaker one, and the country is quick to notice.

!!! example "Watches and hexes"
    On a hex map of 6-mile hexes, a watch of road travel is one hex. An unencumbered party covers four hexes a day, a lightly encumbered one three, a heavily encumbered one two. Off the road, a hex takes more than one watch — or, deep in mountain and jungle, it simply takes as long as the Referee says it does, since the going is decided more by the ground than the pace.

## Actions

**Travel.** The party covers its [miles per watch](Time%20and%20Movement.md#miles-per-watch) for the terrain and weather.

**Explore.** Rather than passing through, the party searches the country around it — ravines off the path, the far side of a ridge, a stream followed to its source. The Referee reveals what a passing party would not see: tracks, ruins, lairs, springs, and anything else the land keeps to itself. In hex terms, the party learns more of the hex it is in.

**Forage.** Each foraging Character makes one [Bushcraft](Skills.md#bushcraft) roll to find food or water. A party can forage for the first watch or two and travel the rest of the day, or forage all day and not move a step.

**Rest.** See below. Active watches can be spent resting too, which is what a [full day of rest](Healing.md) is.

**Anything else** — laying an ambush, following a trail, performing a ritual, visiting a village off the road — takes one watch, or as many as the Referee judges the business worth.

## Rest

The day ends with two watches of rest. Each Character spends **one of them asleep** and has **one free for a single light action** of their own. Making camp, cooking, eating, and the rest of the day's upkeep are already part of this watch; the free action is something easy alongside them — a ritual, a bargain struck in the village, mending gear, or standing guard. Anything that would fill an active watch — travelling, exploring, foraging, digging — does not fit: a Character who spends a rest watch on it is on a [forced march](Time%20and%20Movement.md#forced-march).

**Guard.** A camp is watched only in the watches in which someone spends their free action on guard, so two guards, one in each watch, cover the whole night. An unguarded camp may be Surprised, and a sleeping Character wakes to whatever has already arrived. Standing guard does not spoil a [night's rest](Healing.md).

## Sequence of Play, per Watch

1. **Decide actions:** the players declare what each Character does — for travel, the direction and destination.

2. **Losing direction:** on every second watch of travel, the Referee determines whether the party becomes lost (see below).

3. **Wandering monsters:** on every second watch, the Referee makes a Wandering Encounter check.

4. **Description:** the Referee describes the country passed through and anything of interest found in it, asking for the party's actions as needed. If creatures are met, follow the [Encounter](../Encounters/index.md) pipeline.

5. **End of watch:** the Referee updates the record — spell durations, light sources, and, at the end of the day, rations eaten and feed consumed.

## Losing Direction

Travelling with a reliable guide, following a road, trail, or river, or heading toward a visible landmark, the party cannot get lost. In trackless country, it can.

On every second watch of travel, the Referee secretly rolls the terrain's lost chance from the table below. Before that, the party's designated navigator — one Character — may make a [Bushcraft](Skills.md#bushcraft) roll; on a success, the party keeps its bearings and no lost roll is made. A hired [Guide](../Retainers/Expanded%20Retainer%20List.md) grants a two-point bonus to this Bushcraft roll.

| Terrain | Lost |
| ------------------ | ------ |
| Clear, Plains | 1 in 6 |
| Forest | 2 in 6 |
| Hills | 2 in 6 |
| Desert | 3 in 6 |
| Mountains | 2 in 6 |
| Jungle | 3 in 6 |
| Swamp | 3 in 6 |

**Effects of being lost:** the Referee decides how far off course the party is drifting — commonly 60 degrees left or right of the intended heading, determined at random — and the party does not immediately know it. It may take until a landmark fails to appear, or the sun rises on the wrong side of the trail, for anyone to notice. Correcting course is a matter of play, not a die roll: climbing for a vantage, backtracking, or following water downstream all work as well as they would in life.

## Wandering Monsters

The Referee makes a Wandering Encounter check on every second watch, the watches of rest included, with an encounter occurring on 1 in 6. The more watches a party is on the move, the more it meets. The chance shifts with the country and the party's conduct:

- **Roads** carry traffic — merchants, patrols, pilgrims, and those who prey on them — and are met on more often than open country. A party that leaves the road for the trackless waste pays in speed, but meets less.

- **Dangerous country** — a monster's hunting ground, a haunted forest, the borders of a warlord's land — warrants a higher chance, and the Referee may check every watch.

- **Noise and numbers** — a large party, a train of mules, a fight — raise the chance, at the Referee's discretion.

- **Fire at night** draws everything for miles, the more so in open country, where it can be seen from every hill. A cold, dark camp is hard to find: its chance is 0 in 6, meaning an encounter occurs only if the Referee rolls a 1 twice in a row.

Encounters in the open begin further apart than underground: use [Encounter Distance](../Encounters/Encounter%20Distance.md), extended up to threefold in open terrain, or as far as sight lines allow. If either side is Surprised, they begin close.

## Long Journeys

On a journey of weeks, playing out every watch is more bookkeeping than the trip deserves. The Referee may resolve a day at once: the players declare how the day is spent, and the Referee rolls all its lost rolls and Wandering Encounter checks together — one of each per two watches, as above — placing any encounter in the watch it fell in. Play drops back to single watches the moment something happens.

## Watches in Town

Time in town runs on watches too, and each errand — finding a buyer, asking around the taverns, getting a hearing at the guildhall — takes one. The day holds the watches the party's Encumbrance allows, and the most burdened member sets it for everyone, whether the party walks the streets together or each goes their own way.

This is the reason to leave the armour and the backpack at the inn. A Character who insists on clanking to the market in plate gets through one errand a day, and so does everyone else: plate is slow to walk in and slow to get out of, every gate warden and magistrate's man wants a word with an armed stranger, and someone must stay by the fool to talk the guards out of arresting him.

## Visibility

In open country a Character can see about three miles around. Forest and broken ground cut this to whatever the trees allow; a hilltop or mountainside extends it many times over. Smoke, dust, and large parties on the move are visible much further than the people who made them.

## Resting on the Road

After six days of travel, Characters must rest for a full day, or suffer a −1 penalty to attack and damage rolls until they take one. Recovery of Wounds and Stamina during rest is covered in [Healing](Healing.md).
