# Wilderness Travel

Beyond the walls, time is counted in days. Distances covered, terrain, weather, and encumbrance are handled in [Time and Movement](Time%20and%20Movement.md); finding food and water is the [Bushcraft](Skills.md#bushcraft) skill. This page gives the day its structure — and its ways of going wrong.

## Sequence of Play, per Day

1. **Decide course:** the players declare the day's direction and destination.

2. **Losing direction:** the Referee determines whether the party becomes lost (see below).

3. **Wandering monsters:** the Referee makes any Wandering Encounter checks due.

4. **Description:** the Referee describes the country passed through and anything of interest found in it, asking for the party's actions as needed. If creatures are met, follow the [Encounter](../Encounters/index.md) pipeline.

5. **End of day:** the Referee updates the record — rations eaten, feed consumed, spell durations, and the party's need to rest.

## Losing Direction

Travelling with a reliable guide, following a road, trail, or river, or heading toward a visible landmark, the party cannot get lost. In trackless country, it can.

At the start of each travel day, the Referee secretly rolls the terrain's lost chance from the table below. Before that, the party's designated navigator — one Character — may make a [Bushcraft](Skills.md#bushcraft) roll; on a success, the party keeps its bearings and no lost roll is made. A hired [Guide](../Retainers/Expanded%20Retainer%20List.md) grants a two-point bonus to this Bushcraft roll.

| Terrain | Lost | Encounter |
| ------------------ | ------ | --------- |
| Clear, Plains | 1 in 6 | 1 in 6 |
| Forest | 2 in 6 | 2 in 6 |
| Hills | 2 in 6 | 2 in 6 |
| Desert | 3 in 6 | 2 in 6 |
| Mountains | 2 in 6 | 3 in 6 |
| Jungle | 3 in 6 | 3 in 6 |
| Swamp | 3 in 6 | 3 in 6 |

**Effects of being lost:** the Referee decides how far off course the party is drifting — commonly 60 degrees left or right of the intended heading, determined at random — and the party does not immediately know it. It may take until a landmark fails to appear, or the sun rises on the wrong side of the trail, for anyone to notice. Correcting course is a matter of play, not a die roll: climbing for a vantage, backtracking, or following water downstream all work as well as they would in life.

## Wandering Monsters

The Referee typically makes one Wandering Encounter check per day — around noon or in the evening — at the terrain's encounter chance from the table above. Dangerous or well-hunted country may warrant two or three checks a day, at the Referee's discretion.

Encounters in the open begin further apart than underground: use [Encounter Distance](../Encounters/Encounter%20Distance.md), extended up to threefold in open terrain, or as far as sight lines allow. If either side is Surprised, they begin close.

## Visibility

In open country a Character can see about three miles around. Forest and broken ground cut this to whatever the trees allow; a hilltop or mountainside extends it many times over. Smoke, dust, and large parties on the move are visible much further than the people who made them.

## Resting on the Road

After six days of travel, Characters must rest for a full day, or suffer a −1 penalty to attack and damage rolls until they take one. Recovery of Wounds and Stamina during rest is covered in [Healing](Healing.md); the forced march, and what it costs in flesh, is in [Time and Movement](Time%20and%20Movement.md).
