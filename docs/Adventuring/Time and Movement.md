# Time and Movement

## Time

Within the game, time passes as is convenient for play. If the characters are simply waiting for whatever reason, then the Referee can say, “Two days pass,” or whatever is needed. If nothing of importance happens, then players merely mark off their characters’ food supplies or upkeep costs as appropriate, and handle any other business that occurs during that time.

There are periods when keeping a strict record of time is important. Three units of time have special meaning in the game.

The “Watch” is a share of the day: the day is divided into watches, and each Character does one thing per watch — travels, explores, forages, rests, or goes about some errand. How many watches a day holds depends on how burdened the party is (see [Movement](#movement) below), so a watch runs from four hours for the unencumbered to eight for those staggering under their load. The watch is the clock of the wilderness and of days spent in town; see [Wilderness Travel](Wilderness%20Travel.md).

The “Turn” is ten minutes long. This is often used as a measure of time for spell durations and underground exploring.

The “Round” is ten seconds long. This is primarily used for time management during combat and for the duration of combat-oriented spells.

## Encumbrance
A character’s movement rate is determined by how much gear he is carrying, or how much he is encumbered. The Referee has the authority to call an audit of a character’s inventory at any time, so each player should make sure that their character is carrying a reasonable amount of equipment and that all of the equipment fits somewhere on the character or in a pack. Worn items, such as cloaks, jewellery, backpacks, etc., do not themselves count as items for encumbrance purposes. Carried sacks full of stuff count as an oversize item. 100 coins count as one regular item.

| Criterion                                               | Effect    |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | --------- |
| Character is wearing metal armour                        | +1 Point  |
| Three-quarter or heavier armour                          | +2 Points |
| Per 5 + Strength modifier items the Character is carrying | +1 Point |
| Character is carrying an oversize item                  | +1 Point  |

Very small single items do not count for encumbrance purposes. Multiple small items of the same type (spikes, arrows, etc.) count as one item. All weapons count as separate items. Oversize items are counted separately, +1 Point each, whatever the character's Strength. The Strength modifier changes only how many ordinary items make a Point: a character with Strength 3 gains a Point for every 2 items, one with Strength 18 for every 8. Such items include great and other two handed weapons, and any item that requires two hands to carry or is as tall as the carrying character.

## Movement
Character's on-foot speed depends on the Encumbrance. Any group of Characters moves at the speed of the slowest group member.

Encumbrance also sets how many **active watches** the day holds — watches for travelling, exploring, foraging, and every other business. Every day adds two watches of rest to these, so an unencumbered party's day is six watches of four hours, and a heavily encumbered one's is four watches of six. The burdened walk as far per watch as anyone; they simply have fewer watches in them, and need longer to recover. The party's day is set by its most encumbered member, for everyone.

| Encumbrance Points | Encumbrance         | Active Watches per Day | Movement per Day, Miles | Movement per Turn | Movement per round |         |
| ------------------ | ------------------- | ---------------------- | ----------------------- | ----------------- | ------------------ | ------- |
|                    |                     |                        |                         |                   | Combat             | Running |
| 0-1                | Unencumbered        | 4                      | 24                      | 120'              | 40'                | 120'    |
| 2                  | Lightly Encumbered  | 3                      | 18                      | 90'               | 30'                | 90'     |
| 3                  | Heavily Encumbered  | 2                      | 12                      | 60'               | 20'                | 60'     |
| 4                  | Severely Encumbered | 1                      | 6                       | 30'               | 10'                | 30'     |
| 5+                 | Overencumbered      | 0                      | 0                       | 0'                | 0'                 | 0'      |

### Mounts
If an animal is pulling a cart or other vehicle, the load of the vehicle (as per this scheme) is applied to the animal as well. Multiple mounts pulling a vehicle divide the total encumbrance points between them. Mounts' Encumbrance is determined as follows:

| Criteria                                | Encumbrance                              |
| --------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Every Human riding the animal           | +5 Points and the rider's Encumbrance    |
| Animal is wearing Leather Barding       | +1 Point                                 |
| Chain Barding                           | +2 Points                                |
| Plate Barding                           | +3 Points                                |
| Animal is pulling a vehicle             | -10 Points against vehicle load per axle |
| Per 5 items the animal is carrying      | +1 Point                                 |
| A Teamster has packed the animal's load | -5 Points                                |

Mules subtract five points from their encumbrance load, but move at half the listed speeds. Ponies move at the listed speeds, but automatically start with 6 points of Encumbrance. A mounted party's day holds as many active watches as its most burdened animal — or its most burdened walker, if anyone goes on foot.

### Forced March
Characters can choose to travel through their watches of rest, or to spend them on any other business that would fill an active watch — exploring, foraging, digging. Each rest watch spent this way adds an active watch to the day, and inflicts damage on every Character and animal that works through it, by their Encumbrance:

| Encumbrance         | Damage per Watch Marched |
| ------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Unencumbered        | 1d3                      |
| Lightly Encumbered  | 1d4                      |
| Heavily Encumbered  | 1d6                      |
| Severely Encumbered | 1d8                      |

A Character who marches through their watch of sleep has also gone without it — see [Sleep Deprivation](Hazards/Physical%20Deterioration.md#sleep-deprivation) — and a night spent marching is no night's rest for [Healing](Healing.md). An animal that suffers 5 or more points of damage in this manner in a single day dies at the end of that day's journey.

### Excavations
A single man can excavate 12 cubic feet of earth per watch if they have proper equipment, adjusted by 3 cubic feet per point of Strength modifier. They can dig at half of this rate if they have improvised tools, and one quarter of this rate if they have no tools at all.
