# Dungeon Exploration

Underground and indoor exploration — ruins, crypts, mines, cellars, and every other place the sun does not reach — is played out in Turns of ten minutes. The Turn is the clock of the dungeon: it burns the torches, empties the waterskins, and brings the things that live below out to see who is making all that noise.

## Sequence of Play, per Turn

1. **Wandering monsters:** the Referee makes a Wandering Encounter check, if one is due.

2. **Actions:** the party decides what it does this Turn — moving, searching, listening, forcing a door, entering a room.

3. **Description:** the Referee describes what happens. If creatures are met, follow the [Encounter](../Encounters/index.md) pipeline.

4. **End of Turn:** the Referee updates the time record, with special attention to light sources, spell durations, and the party's need to rest.

## Movement

When exploring unknown areas, Characters cover their movement rate in feet per Turn — see [Time and Movement](Time%20and%20Movement.md) for rates by encumbrance. The pace accounts for darkness, caution, mapping, watching the floor for tripwires and the ceiling for worse, and being able to react when something happens.

In areas the party already knows, the Referee may allow faster movement — three times the exploring rate is reasonable. Running is possible, and is what [Flee!](../Encounters/Combat%20Actions.md#flee) is for.

## Resting

After five Turns of exploration, Characters must rest for one Turn. A party that presses on without resting suffers a −1 penalty to attack and damage rolls until it rests for a full Turn.

This is distinct from the one-hour rest that restores Stamina — six uninterrupted Turns, with a Wandering Encounter check for each; see [Healing](Healing.md). Neither restores anything if interrupted by unwelcome company.

## Light and Darkness

Most Characters see nothing underground without a light source, and the light that saves them also announces them — see the note on clunking brutes under [Surprise](../Encounters/Surprise.md), and the eyes-in-the-dark warning under [Encounter Distance](../Encounters/Encounter%20Distance.md). Fighting in darkness is covered in [Combat Actions](../Encounters/Combat%20Actions.md).

| Light source | Radius | Burns for |
| ------------ | ------ | --------- |
| Candle | 10' | 12 Turns (2 hours) |
| Torch | 30' | 6 Turns (1 hour) |
| Lantern | 30' | 24 Turns (4 hours) per flask of oil |

A torch dropped or used as a weapon has a 1 in 2 chance of going out. A lantern's shutters can hide its light without extinguishing the flame.

## Doors

Dungeon doors are rarely cooperative. Locked and stuck doors are handled by the [Doors](Skills.md#doors) skill and Tinkering; breaking them down takes time and the right tool, as described there.

**Listening at doors:** in a quiet moment, any Character has a 1 in 6 chance to make out faint sounds through a door — muttering, shuffling, the click of claws. The Referee always rolls this in secret, so the listener never knows whether the roll failed or there is simply nothing to hear. Each Character gets one attempt per door. Some things — the patient dead among them — make no sound at all.

**A failed attempt to force a door** ends any chance of Surprise against whatever waits on the other side.

## Searching

Secret doors, hidden compartments, and concealed mechanisms are found with the [Search](Skills.md#search) skill — one Turn per 10' of area per Character, rolled by the Referee in secret. Traps are covered under [Environmental](Hazards/Environmental.md) hazards. Finding a secret door does not automatically reveal how it opens.

## Wandering Monsters

Beyond the creatures the Referee has placed, dungeons have traffic: patrols, scavengers, and things drawn by noise. The Referee typically makes a Wandering Encounter check every second Turn, with an encounter occurring on 1 in 6. Both cadence and chance may vary by area — a busy warren may warrant a check every Turn at 2 in 6, a sealed tomb one check an hour.

- **Noise and light** — battle, hammering at doors, a blazing lantern — raise the chance, at the Referee's discretion.

- **Hiding quietly** in an out-of-the-way spot warrants only infrequent checks.

- Wandering monsters arrive from a direction of the Referee's choosing, moving toward the party; determine the starting gap with [Encounter Distance](../Encounters/Encounter%20Distance.md) — underground, visibility is the ceiling.

- Wanderers are met on the move, away from their lairs and their hoards. They carry danger and, usually, nothing worth dying for.

## Dungeon Levels

Dungeons descend in floors, called levels, and deeper is worse. As a rule of thumb, a dungeon level presents dangers suited to Characters of equal level, and creatures tend to lair at the depth matching their Hit Dice. The stairs down are always an invitation and always a question: the treasure below is richer, and so is the graveyard.
