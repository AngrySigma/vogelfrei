# Substance and Toxins

### Drugs & Alcohol
Drugs are treated much as poison would be, but the effects of a failed saving throw (no save if intentionally using drugs) are usually much less severe than death. The Referee will determine the effects of specific drugs in his campaign.

Alcohol is far more pernicious, as it is quite common for most everyone to use it to excess, as beer and wine are often safer to drink than plain water. Drunk characters are –2 to Agility and all saving throws.

---
### Poison
There are a variety of ways that a character can become poisoned. When exposed to poison, the character must make a saving throw against Poison or suffer the effects. Typical effects of a poison are instant death, falling into a deep slumber, the loss of Wounds, or perhaps the reduction in one or more ability scores. Only all-or-nothing effects are considered to be poison. Poisons that cause gradual or incremental effects are considered to be drugs for rules purposes.

Poison bypasses Stamina entirely; see [Damage](Damage.md).

---
### Poisons for Purchase

At the Referee's option, manufactured poisons may be available for purchase. Their sale is prohibited nearly everywhere, and using them is the kind of act that gets a person hanged twice; buyers must turn to the black market, to certain apothecaries who ask no questions, or to guilds whose trade this is. Prices below are city black-market rates — where such a market exists at all.

Each poison is described by:

- **Save Modifier** — applied to the victim's saving throw against Poison. A higher modifier means an easier save and a weaker toxin.

- **Detection** — the chance of the victim noticing the poison: visually, per Round a poisoned blade is in use, or by smell and taste for something slipped into food or drink. A victim who notices a tainted meal refuses it; one who spots a greased blade may raise an alarm, or worse.

- **Onset** — the delay before the effect strikes. "Instant" means before the victim can react in any way.

- **Effect** — what a failed (and, for ingested poisons, a successful) save brings.

#### Bloodstream Poisons

Applied to a bladed weapon; delivered by a wound.

| Type | Cost | Save Mod. | Detection | Onset | Save Succeeds | Save Fails |
| ---- | ------ | --------- | --------- | ------------ | ------------- | ---------- |
| I | 10sp | +6 | 8 in 10 | 1d4+1 Rounds | None | 1d6 damage |
| II | 75sp | +5 | 6 in 10 | 1d3 Rounds | None | 2d6 damage |
| III | 600sp | +4 | 4 in 10 | 1 Round | None | 3d6 damage |
| IV | 1500sp | +3 | 1 in 10 | Instant | None | Death |

**Application:** applying a vial to a weapon takes one Turn. One vial covers a single bladed melee weapon, or up to 6 arrows or bolts.

**Effectiveness:** once applied, a poison lasts two days or two hits in combat, whichever comes first. After one day, or after the first successful hit, it drops to half effectiveness: half damage, or a +4 bonus to the save for poisons that kill outright. After that it is spent.

#### Antidotes

Where a poison can be bought, so usually can its antidote, at the same price as a dose of the poison itself. An antidote fully negates the poison's effects if administered within the onset time — for instant poisons, within the same Round. A character with the [Herbalism](../Skills.md#herbalism) skill may roll to recognize a poison or an antidote for what it is.
