- **High — Epoch (source lines 35–68):** Chariot, trireme, quadrireme, and Viking-style longship are ancient/medieval rather than 17th-century European/New World vehicle types. Triremes and quadriremes are specifically ships of antiquity ([trireme history](https://www.worldhistory.org/trireme/)); Royal Museums Greenwich places longships in the Viking/medieval sequence ([shipbuilding overview](https://www.rmg.co.uk/stories/maritime-history/shipbuilding-800-1800)). Replace them with period regional craft unless a deliberate archaeological survival is explained.
- **Medium — Epoch (source lines 87–99):** Cog, caravel, and carrack are presented beside 17th-century types without noting that they are principally medieval/15th-century developments; the list needs a defined date/region or period-appropriate replacements ([Royal Museums Greenwich](https://www.rmg.co.uk/stories/maritime-history/shipbuilding-800-1800)).
- **High — Rules (source lines 6–127):** Vehicles have prices but no capacity, crew, speed, seaworthiness, durability, or combat data, so the movement/encumbrance rules cannot actually resolve their use.
- **Medium — Rules (source lines 129–130):** Quality premiums (“Good,” “Excellent,” “Advanced,” “Poor,” “Awful”) have no defined mechanical or fictional effect; “Advanced” is also not a quality grade.
